# Prompt library: trackers, planners, calendars and student lists

Ready to paste into the Factory's Brief box and the three brief fields on the New product window. This page is a companion to [Presets and the Brief box](WALKTHROUGH.md#20-presets-and-the-brief-box). Every card uses a product type the Factory really has, and names the preset to start from where one exists. Where there is no preset, the card says so.

## How to use these

1. Open the Library, pick the product type, and choose the preset named on the card (if there is one). Then change the Settings to the values on the card.
2. Fill the three brief fields: **Who it is for** (pick a line from the audience list below), **Problem it solves**, and **What makes it different** (both are on the card). Each field is limited to 200 characters.
3. Paste the card's **Brief** into the Brief box (600 characters at most).
4. Set the license tier from the card, and tick Month tabs on dated planners and calendars if the buyer will use a tablet or read the PDF on screen.

## What a brief can and can't do

- It only steers the AI's wording: the content the AI writes inside the product, and the listing copy. It does not change layout, page counts or sections. Those come from the type's Settings.
- Mandalas, mazes, geometric pattern art and imported coloring pages have no AI-written content, so for those the brief only shapes the listing copy.
- It does nothing unless an AI key is set up. With no key the Factory uses its built-in content library and ignores the brief.
- The three fields join the Brief into labelled lines for the AI. Presets carry only the single Brief string. They do not fill the three fields or set the license tier, so you fill those yourself each time.
- Each **What makes it different** line below says only what the finished product literally contains. Do not add claims about apps, Goodnotes, tablet paper sizes, prioritisation or workload features. The Factory cannot produce those yet.
- The briefs rule out medical, legal, tax, investment and results claims, and real brand names, where they fit that product. Keep those lines in if you edit a brief.

## Who it is for (pick one per product)

- **College students:** College students juggling four or five classes, each with its own deadlines and grading
- **School students:** School students aged 11 to 18 who have to keep track of homework across many subjects
- **Teachers:** Classroom teachers planning a school year around lessons, marking, parent contact and a little rest
- **Busy parents:** Busy parents running school runs, meals, activities and appointments for a whole family
- **Solo business owners:** Solo business owners and side-hustlers who track their work on paper because they have no software or accountant
- **ADHD-friendly:** People with ADHD who give up on paper systems that are too big, too strict or full of guilt
- **Seniors:** Older adults who want large, clear print and plenty of room to write by hand
- **Kids activities:** Parents and teachers of children aged 4 to 8 who want printable activities with an answer key
- **General adults:** Adults who want a simple, calm paper system and do not want to set up an app

## Trackers

### Habit tracker

- **Product type:** habit-tracker
- **Preset to start from:** Student habit tracker; ADHD-friendly habit tracker
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for a general-adult or small-business habit tracker.
- **Settings:** months 12, rows 8 to 12 (fewer rows for the ADHD version).
- **Good audiences:** College students / School students / ADHD-friendly / General adults
- **License tier:** Personal
- **Problem it solves** (111/200): Habits get dropped because tracking sheets are too long and nobody notices a missed day until the month is over
- **What makes it different** (94/200): Monthly pages with suggested habits plus blank rows for your own, and a monthly reflection box
- **Brief** (222/600):

  > Habits that take under 10 minutes and can be ticked off yes or no. Kind wording, no streak pressure and no guilt. Reflection prompt is one gentle question. Never name brands or apps. No medical, diet or weight-loss claims.

### Reading log

- **Product type:** reading-log
- **Preset to start from:** Student reading log; Kids reading log
- **Not covered yet:** None for adult readers.
- **Settings:** books 40, goal 24, minutesMonths 3, reviewPages 6 (kids: books 20, minutesMonths 2, reviewPages 4).
- **Good audiences:** School students / General adults
- **License tier:** Personal
- **Problem it solves** (105/200): Reading goals fade because there is nowhere to see progress and no reason to try a different kind of book
- **What makes it different** (143/200): A numbered list for 40 books with star ratings, a reading challenge of mixed kinds of book, monthly reading-minutes pages and book review pages
- **Brief** (246/600):

  > A reading challenge that mixes fiction, nonfiction, short reads and a book outside your usual taste. Review prompts ask what you thought, in plain words. Never name real books, authors, series or publishers. No claims about test scores or grades.

## Planners

- **Product type:** goal-planner
- **Preset to start from:** Student goal planner; Teacher goal planner; Small business goal planner
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for a personal or New Year goal planner.
- **Settings:** goals 3 or 4.
- **Good audiences:** College students / Teachers / Solo business owners / General adults
- **License tier:** Personal

### Weekly planner (dated)

- **Product type:** weekly-planner
- **Preset to start from:** Teacher weekly planner; Busy parent weekly planner
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for students, small business or general adults, and none with Month tabs.
- **Settings:** weeks 52 (teachers 40), weekStart 1 or 0; tick Month tabs for tablet and PDF users. Start date defaults to 1 January of the next year from September.
- **Good audiences:** Teachers / Busy parents / Solo business owners / College students
- **License tier:** Personal (Classroom for a teacher-facing product only if you want that wording)
- **Problem it solves** (117/200): Weeks fill up with small tasks and the one thing that mattered gets lost, so people end the week unsure what they did
- **What makes it different** (119/200): One dated week per spread with a top-priorities box and a weekly focus line, plus clickable month tabs when switched on
- **Brief** (215/600):

  > One short focus line per week, matched to the time of year (school terms, holidays, seasons). Calm and practical, not hustle talk. Never claim the planner will boost productivity or income. Short lines, plain words.

### Daily planner (undated)

- **Product type:** daily-planner
- **Preset to start from:** Undated daily planner page
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for a dated daily planner, and none with Month tabs. Dated is limited to 92 pages and Month tabs need a month start inside the range.
- **Settings:** dated off, days 1, priorities 3; for dated: dated on, a start date, days up to 92.
- **Good audiences:** General adults / ADHD-friendly / College students
- **License tier:** Personal
- **Problem it solves** (87/200): Daily to-do lists get too long to finish, so the important task is buried under errands
- **What makes it different** (100/200): An hourly schedule, three top-priority slots, and to-do, notes and daily wins sections on every page
- **Brief** (222/600):

  > Daily focus questions that are one line and answerable in a sentence. Section titles are short and kind (priorities, to do, notes, wins). Never use shame or pressure words. No productivity, medical or time-saving promises.

## Coloring and activities

### Mandala coloring pages

- **Product type:** mandala-coloring
- **Preset to start from:** None yet
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for this type. The Geometric coloring book preset belongs to a different type, geometric pattern art.
- **Settings:** pages 12, detail detailed (simple, medium, detailed or intricate), motifSet celestial (classic, botanical, geometric or celestial), symmetry auto, cover on.
- **Good audiences:** General adults / Busy parents / Seniors
- **License tier:** Personal
- **What the Brief does here:** The drawings are procedural, so the Brief and the three fields do not change them. They only shape the listing copy. Motif set, Detail and Symmetry do the steering. The celestial set is made of sunburst, diamond, dot-cluster, ring-shell, chevron, teardrop and petal shapes. It cannot draw moons, stars or constellations.
- **Problem it solves** (104/200): Coloring pages are often too crowded or too faint to color, and sets reuse one design with small changes
- **What makes it different** (144/200): Twelve unique mandala designs at detailed level, mirror symmetry, crisp vector line art, and SVG files for digital coloring and cutting machines
- **Brief** (204/600):

  > Calm, descriptive listing copy. Say what is in the set and how to print it. No claims about stress relief, therapy, mental health or mindfulness benefits. No promises of results. No brand or artist names.

### Maze activity book

- **Product type:** maze-activity
- **Preset to start from:** None yet
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for this type. The Geometric coloring book preset belongs to a different type, geometric pattern art.
- **Settings:** pages 10, difficulty easy (easy, medium or hard), solutions on.
- **Good audiences:** Kids activities / School students
- **License tier:** Personal
- **What the Brief does here:** Mazes are procedural, so the Brief only shapes the listing copy. Each maze is a plain grid with a start arrow and an end arrow. There is no space or story art, no astronaut or rocket, and the page titles are Maze 1, Maze 2 and so on. QA proves every maze has exactly one solution.
- **Problem it solves** (94/200): Printable mazes are often too easy or impossible, and a parent cannot check the answer quickly
- **What makes it different** (92/200): Ten mazes at easy difficulty, each verified to have exactly one solution, with an answer key
- **Brief** (226/600):

  > Plain, friendly listing copy for parents and teachers. Say how many mazes, the difficulty and that an answer key is included. Do not mention a story, characters or themed art. No claims about learning, IQ or brain development.

### Word search puzzles

- **Product type:** word-search
- **Preset to start from:** None yet
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for this type. The Geometric coloring book preset belongs to a different type, geometric pattern art.
- **Settings:** puzzles 8, gridSize 10, wordsPerPuzzle 8, difficulty easy, topic "ocean life" (3 to 40 characters), solutions on. Words are 3 to 12 letters, and no longer than the grid.
- **Good audiences:** Kids activities / School students / Teachers
- **License tier:** Personal (Classroom if the listing is aimed at teachers)
- **What the Brief does here:** This is the one activity type where the Brief really steers the content: the AI writes each puzzle's sub-topic and word list. QA checks every word is placed and fits the grid. It cannot judge whether a word suits the age group, so read the word lists.
- **Problem it solves** (99/200): Word searches repeat the same easy words, and many have no clear theme or a hard-to-read answer key
- **What makes it different** (72/200): Eight themed puzzles on 10x10 grids at easy difficulty, with answer keys
- **Brief** (214/600):

  > Each puzzle has one clear sub-topic. Use common words a 6 to 8 year old can spell, 3 to 8 letters. No proper nouns, brand names or frightening words. Do not repeat a word across puzzles. Titles are short and plain.

### Illustrated coloring pages

- **Product type:** illustrated-coloring
- **Preset to start from:** None yet
- **Not covered yet:** No preset for this type. The Geometric coloring book preset belongs to a different type, geometric pattern art.
- **Settings:** pages 8 (up to 20), fit sharp or fill, cover on. Needs an image provider in Settings → AI providers: OpenAI is the default, with ten others and a fallback order.
- **Good audiences:** Kids activities / General adults
- **License tier:** Personal
- **What the Brief does here:** The AI writes a list of scene descriptions (under 80 characters each), and the Brief steers that list only. The picture prompt is built in: thick bold outlines for young children, fine outlines otherwise, white background, no text. QA flags gray tones, color and low resolution. It cannot tell what was drawn, so look at every page for cropped parts and odd faces.
- **Problem it solves** (82/200): Kids coloring pages are often cluttered or have tiny shapes that are hard to color
- **What makes it different** (94/200): Eight illustrated black-and-white line-art pages, with PNG files included for digital coloring
- **Brief** (238/600):

  > One clear main animal or object per scene, with a simple background. Name the exact subject in every scene (turtle, seahorse, crab). Friendly, calm expressions. No text, logos or characters from films, games or books. Nothing frightening.

## Gaps in one place

The Factory has no preset for any of these, so you set the type up by hand. The briefs above still work for them:

- Dated daily planner (the type exists; only the undated preset does)
- Any preset with Month tabs switched on
- Any coloring or activity type (mandala, maze, word search, illustrated coloring)
- A habit tracker for general adults or small businesses
- A weekly planner for students, small businesses or general adults
- Household or freelancer budget planners
- Checklists other than back-to-school (moving, travel, wedding, party)
- Journals other than the 30-day gratitude journal
- Any preset that sets the three brief fields or the license tier

## License tier notes

- Personal is right for nearly everything here.
- Classroom and Commercial wording in Settings → License text are still drafts. QA warns until you edit them. Decide what Commercial allows before you sell anything under it.
- A teacher-facing product only needs Classroom if you want that wording on the listing.

## Not verified

- Which of these will sell is untested. Nothing here is a sales claim.
- No card has been run end to end yet. Make one product from a card and check the wording that comes out before you rely on it.
