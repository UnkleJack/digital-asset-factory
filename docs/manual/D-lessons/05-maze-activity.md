# Lesson 5: Generate and inspect a maze activity

**Verification:** a four-maze product was made and its QA and files read in a throwaway copy (VERIFIED live).

## Learning objective
See that a maze is generated by an algorithm, what QA proves about it, and what only you can check.

## What we are creating
A four-maze pack. This is practice brief 3, *Mission: Space Rescue Maze Pack* ([practice briefs](00-the-twelve-practice-briefs.md)); it does not exist yet.

## What we need
The running app. No AI key.

## Steps
1. New product → **Kids Activities** → **Maze activity book**.
2. **Title:** type `Sample Space Rescue Mazes` yourself (the theme does not change mazes). Pages 4, Difficulty **easy**, Solutions on.
3. Check the **SAMPLE** panel: it shows the first mazes.
4. Create. Wait for Ready.
5. Open the product's **QA** tab.

## Expected results (live)
- PDF of **5 pages**: four mazes plus one answer page (ceil(4 / 4) = 1).
- QA: `artwork_count: 4 mazes for 4 pages`, **`maze_solvable: Every maze is a perfect maze with exactly one solution`**.
- The ZIP holds only the PDF, license, instructions and manifest. The maze SVGs are **internal** and not delivered.

## What to inspect
- Page through: does each answer match its maze (the answer page shows route lines)?
- Is the difficulty right for the age? Easy is a 10 x 13 grid; medium 16 x 21; hard 24 x 32.
- Is there a clear start and finish?

## Common mistakes
Expecting a space look; looking for the SVGs in the ZIP; picking **hard** for young children.

## Completion criteria
- [ ] Product Ready, QA Passed.
- [ ] I checked every answer page against its maze.
- [ ] I know the title is mine to write.

## What I learned
QA proves each maze has exactly one solution. It cannot tell you the maze is fun or the right difficulty.
