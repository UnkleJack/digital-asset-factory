# Lesson 6: Generate and validate a word search

**Verification:** a four-puzzle product was made in a throwaway copy and passed QA (VERIFIED live). Which built-in word list the Ocean theme picked was NOT read back (the match rule is from the code).

## Learning objective
Learn what QA proves about a word search and why **you must read the word lists**.

## What we are creating
*Ocean Explorer Word Search* (practice brief 4; see [practice briefs](00-the-twelve-practice-briefs.md)); it does not exist yet.

## What we need
The running app. No AI key.

## Steps
1. New product → **Kids Activities** → **Word search puzzles**.
2. Title `Sample Ocean Word Search`. **Theme: Ocean.** Puzzles 4, Grid size 14, Words per puzzle 12, Difficulty medium, Solutions on.
   - The **Topic** field is not on the form; the built-in lists are chosen by matching the **Theme** name.
3. Create. Open the product, QA tab.
4. Read the word list on every puzzle page.

## Expected results
- PDF of **6 pages**: 4 puzzle pages plus ceil(4 / 2) = 2 answer pages (live: "PDF opens; 6 pages (expected 6)").
- QA: `words_placed` (every word is in its grid, hard), `word_length` (no word longer than the grid, hard), `content_count`. All passed in the live run.

## What to inspect
- Every word is spelled right and suits the audience.
- No offensive hidden word appears by accident in the letter soup.
- Answer pages highlight the right words.
- Offline, more than 10 puzzles repeat lists.

## Common mistakes
Choosing a theme with no built-in list (for example Celestial), which falls back to **animals**; trusting QA to judge difficulty; hard difficulty (reversed words) for young children.

## Completion criteria
- [ ] QA Passed; I read every list.
- [ ] I know QA proves placement, not suitability.

## What I learned
The grid is always generated by the app. Words come from AI or the built-in list. QA cannot tell whether a word is too hard or odd for the topic.
